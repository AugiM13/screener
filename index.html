Screens odds range, EV floor, books, fees

  
A bet has to clear all of these. Widening them until something appears is how you end up on the rows that only look good.

  

    

    

    

    

    

  

  

    
    
  

  

    
    
    
    
    
  

  

    Platform fees — verify these on each site before trusting a net number
    

      

      

      

      

      

      

      

    

    
Kalshi runs on a curve rather than a flat rate: 7% × price × (1 − price) per contract, which is 7% × (1 − price) of what you put up. A short favorite costs about 2.3%, an even-money bet 3.5%, a +400 longshot 5.6%. The fee is charged on top of the contract cost, so net EV is (gross − fee) ÷ (1 + fee). Kalshi also rounds each order's fee up to the next cent; the board includes that when it counts whole contracts.
Polymarket US uses the same curve at 6.95% (since Sep 17, 2026 — your Oct 25 Kenneth Walker fill paid $0.16 on 9.28 shares at 49¢, which is 6.95%). Novig is free before the game; once it's live, takers pay 6% × price × (1 − price) (from Novig's own market data) — added automatically for live bets in + Add a bet and the bet editor.

  






  
Stake sizing bankroll and Kelly

  
Kelly scales a bet to its edge. The haircut shrinks that edge first, because the fair values behind it move and the biggest ones move most. What survives gets clamped to the range you actually bet in.

  

    

    

    

    

    

    

    

    

    

    

  

  
One daily max for everything (v3.11): the API board, the pasted Market EV page and exchange fills all count against the same %, so a big paste can't stack on top of the board. Stakes size off a blend: the market's no-vig price pulled toward BettingPros' number by the model weight (0.35 = mostly market). If there's no market price for a bet, the model number is used with the haircut. Bets on a game you already have money on are trimmed so the game stays under the per-game max.

Lines

  
"Edge" is the real answer to "is this good value": a fair win probability with the vig stripped out, compared against what this specific book is offering. Pinnacle is the sharp reference (its line devigged, pulled via pinnapi.com); Consensus is the devigged average of every book we pull, and is the fallback when Pinnacle data isn't available. BettingPros' own EV/Cover % (its model's opinion) are shown for reference only.

Edges are net of fees, using the same fee inputs as the Board tab (Kalshi's price-based curve, the rest as you've set them) — hover an edge to see its gross and fee. Only games that haven't kicked off are shown. Pinnacle's line is a pre-game line, and comparing it against live in-play prices produces fake edges (a game 80 minutes in once showed a 168% "edge"). Stale quotes are dropped automatically, and any edge over 10% is flagged with ⚠ — on a moneyline that's almost always a bad quote, so confirm the price on the book before betting.

CLV here compares your price to the closing price on the same side, in implied-probability terms. It is not devigged, so a positive number is evidence rather than proof — but a screen that consistently beats the close is finding real prices, and one that does not is finding noise no matter what the EV column said.

  
Expect a few hundred bets before any of this separates from variance.
